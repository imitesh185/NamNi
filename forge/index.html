The Forge

Where the Atlas buys breadth at commander-level, this buys depth in one profession — deep enough to defend architectural decisions under hostile questioning, and deep enough that the answer to "why did you do it that way" is a mechanism rather than a convention.

The organising principle. Every branch is entered through a system that breaks in a specific, reproducible way. The concept is the fix, not the starting point. This is the same discipline as your projects track — claim, measurement, surprise — applied to the tools you already use professionally but may not have taken apart.
#spark-internals #kafka-internals #stream-semantics #platform #labs-not-courses

A dependency graph with a lab attached to every load-bearing node.

The bar this is calibrated to Not "can you use Spark" — "can you explain why your job took 40 minutes, show me in the UI where the time went, and tell me what you would change first and why." That question separates five years of usage from five years of understanding, and it is the actual difference the target companies are screening for.

01 What five years of experience changes

The tree is shaped differently for you than it would be for someone starting out.

You have almost certainly used most of L1 and much of L2 and L5. What experience does not reliably produce is internals, because production work rewards making things work rather than understanding why they work. So each branch below carries a status:

StatusMeaningHow to treat it
ownYou likely hold this at working levelVerify by explaining it cold, then skip. Do not re-learn.
deepenYou use it; the internals are probably fuzzyThis is where most of your time goes. Labs, not reading.
newGenuinely likely to be new territoryEnter through the lab, not the documentation.
The verification move, used once per branch Before starting any branch, write the one-paragraph explanation from memory, closed-book. Where it comes out fluent and specific, mark it own and move on. Where it comes out vague or reaches for vocabulary instead of mechanism, that is deepen. Two months is only enough if you refuse to re-cover ground you already hold.

02 What those companies actually test

This shapes the weighting of the whole tree, so it comes before the tree.

The target set is not homogeneous, and knowing the difference tells you which branches carry the most weight.

Company shapeWhat the data problem actually isBranches weighted heaviest

Those four branches are where a five-year candidate is separated from a two-year candidate, and they are where this tree concentrates its depth. Everything else is either supporting or differentiating for a specific company shape.

L0 The substrate expanded

deepen Small on purpose. Only what genuinely gates something above it. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. Four things in L0 explain a disproportionate share of everything above: columnar layout explains why analytics is fast, the WAL is what CDC reads, atomic pointer swap is how table formats get ACID on storage that has none, and idempotency is the only durable answer to partial failure.

PREREQUISITES. None. This is the floor.

LAB FAMILY. Format forensics — take a real dataset and measure what each layout, encoding and sort order actually costs. Then rebuild a table format's atomic commit from scratch and prove it survives concurrent readers.

Connects out Columnar layout → L2 Spark I/O · WAL → L5 CDC · atomic swap → L6 correctness · idempotency → L4 exactly-once and L5 backfills.

L1 Data architecture & modelling expanded

own mostly — with two sub-branches marked deepen. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. This is where five years of experience shows most, and also where it is most often shallow in one specific place: grain. Choosing the wrong grain is the only modelling decision that is genuinely unrecoverable, and most engineers have never had to articulate why.

PREREQUISITES. L0 storage formats, for the physical design sub-branch.

THE TWO TO DEEPEN. Slowly changing dimensions beyond type 2 — most people know type 2 and stop, and types 4 and 6 come up in interviews precisely because they separate people who read a blog from people who have modelled a real business. And physical design — partitioning, clustering, file sizing — because it is where modelling meets L2 performance.

LAB FAMILY. A modelling case study where the requirement changes four times, and each change exposes a different failure of the naive model. The fourth change forces temporal modelling.

L2 Apache Spark expanded

deepen — the largest branch, and the highest-yield one. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. Every interview at every company in your target set will go deep here, and the questions are almost always the same shape: this job is slow, why, and what do you change? Answering that requires the shuffle, the memory model, the plan, and the UI — in that order.

PREREQUISITES. L0 (formats, distributed minimum, Python memory). Nothing else.

THE SPINE OF THIS BRANCH. The shuffle. Stage boundaries, skew, join strategy, AQE, memory pressure and spill all radiate from it. If you understand shuffle completely, most of the rest of Spark follows.

LAB FAMILY. Four separate laboratories: shuffle and skew; the query optimiser as an observable object; memory and spill; and join strategy selection. Each is entered by building something that is slow for a reason you must discover in the UI rather than be told.

The trap in this branch Reading about Catalyst and AQE without watching them act. Every concept here has an observable consequence in the Spark UI or in an explain plan — and the interview question is always "how would you see that", not "what is that".

L3 Apache Kafka expanded

deepen — second largest, and the one with the most operational questions. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. Kafka questions in senior interviews are overwhelmingly about failure and operation, not about the API: what happens when a consumer dies, what causes lag, what does acks=all actually guarantee, why did partition count change break ordering, what is a rebalance storm.

PREREQUISITES. L0 distributed minimum. Independent of L2 — these two branches do not gate each other and can be taken in either order.

THE SPINE OF THIS BRANCH. The partition as the unit of everything — ordering, parallelism, assignment, replication and lag are all partition-scoped, and nearly every Kafka behaviour that surprises people follows from that.

LAB FAMILY. Four laboratories: partitioning and key distribution; consumer group rebalancing; replication and broker failure; delivery semantics and duplicate injection. Every one is built around deliberately breaking a running cluster and observing what the clients do.

L4 Stream processing semantics expanded

new for most people, including experienced ones. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. This is the branch that most reliably separates candidates. Almost everyone can describe a Kafka consumer; far fewer can explain what a watermark is actually guessing about, or what "exactly-once" requires from a sink, or why their unbounded stream-stream join is a memory leak.

PREREQUISITES. L3 (you need a real event stream), L0 idempotency. L2 only if implementing in Structured Streaming rather than Flink.

THE ENTRY PROBLEM. Build an hourly aggregation over a stream where 5% of events arrive late. Your counts are wrong, and they were already reported. Everything in this branch — event time, windows, watermarks, allowed lateness, restatement — arrives as a response to that single reproducible failure.

LAB FAMILY. Three laboratories: the watermark and late-data lab; the stateful processing and state-growth lab; and the end-to-end exactly-once lab where you kill the job mid-stream and prove no loss and no duplication by count.

L5 Pipelines & orchestration expanded

own in practice — deepen on CDC and backfills. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. You have built pipelines for five years. The two places experience frequently leaves gaps are CDC correctness — deletes, out-of-order updates, watermark advancing past a slow transaction — and backfill economics, where the interview question is about reprocessing two years of history when the source schema has changed twice since.

PREREQUISITES. L0 WAL for CDC. L1 modelling for incremental design.

LAB FAMILY. Two laboratories: an idempotency lab where running the same job twice doubles everything until you fix it properly; and a CDC lab where you lose rows three distinct ways before reading the WAL directly.

L6 Reliability & governance expanded

deepen — and the branch that most differentiates for SaaS and payments companies. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. The defining asymmetry of data engineering: a broken service returns an error, a broken pipeline returns plausible numbers. Everything here is defence against that, and it is what a company means when it says it wants someone senior.

PREREQUISITES. L5 (you need pipelines to instrument).

LAB FAMILY. Two laboratories: a data quality lab where you introduce four faults that all leave the pipeline green; and a lineage and contracts prototype where a schema change is caught before it ships rather than after it breaks a dashboard.

L7 Data system design expanded

new as a disciplined practice, even if you have designed systems. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. This is the round you will be judged on most heavily for a senior role, and it is a performance as much as a knowledge test — the ability to move from vague requirements to a defended design in 45 minutes, out loud, while being interrupted.

PREREQUISITES. L1 through L6. This branch is a synthesis and cannot usefully come earlier.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT FROM GENERIC SYSTEM DESIGN. The axes are throughput, latency, correctness, schema evolution, cost and recovery — not QPS and load balancers. The questions are about the data's characteristics: volume, velocity, cardinality, skew, lateness, mutability and retention.

LAB FAMILY. Seven written design documents, each following the same structure, each with an explicit failure-mode section. These become portfolio artifacts directly.

L8 Data platform engineering expanded

new — the destination, and the thing that changes your title. Full branch →

WHY IT MATTERS. The shift from building pipelines to building the thing other people build pipelines on. The mental change is from solving a problem to solving a class of problems for people you will never meet, which is a product discipline as much as an engineering one.

PREREQUISITES. All of L1–L7. This is genuinely last.

THE ORGANISING QUESTION. How does a company let three hundred people work with data without three hundred bespoke pipelines and three hundred conflicting definitions of "active user"? Every sub-branch — catalog, contracts, self-service, golden paths, chargeback — is an answer to some part of that.

LAB FAMILY. One deliberately scoped mini-platform: an ingestion path that a hypothetical other team can use without talking to you, with a catalog, a contract check, a quality gate and a cost attribution. Small in scale, complete in shape.

Scope decision — all nine branches, full depth The timeline is yours to manage and it flexes. Every branch was therefore expanded to full depth, including L6 reliability and L8 platform, rather than being truncated to fit a fixed window. The dependency order in traversal is what actually matters; the calendar is not part of this document. Nothing later depends on finishing anything earlier quickly — only on finishing it properly.
How to use it from here Open one branch. Read its one question and its environment section. Build the environment before reading any nodes — the labs are the point, and every branch's environment section exists because the setup determines whether the labs teach anything.

Then work the nodes in the order of that branch's internal dependency map, and stop at the first lab. Reading further before running it converts the branch into an article.
The recurrences worth watching for Four ideas appear in three or more branches, and noticing them is worth more than any single fact. The power law — Spark skew, Kafka hot partitions, streaming hot keys, SaaS tenant sizes. Write-elsewhere-then-flip-one-pointer — atomic commit, backfills, blue-green deployment. Event time versus processing time — watermarks and bi-temporal modelling, discovered independently by two traditions. And "when not to" — S13, K12, W12, P12, E11: five nodes across five branches that are one professional disposition.
