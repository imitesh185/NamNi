Physics & the Physical World

Physics is the domain that tells you what is possible. Every engineering constraint in this Atlas — why batteries are hard, why chips get hot, why latency has a floor, why an engine cannot exceed a certain efficiency — is a physics fact wearing an engineering costume.

The single idea. A small number of quantities are conserved — energy, momentum, charge — and a smaller number of things always increase. Almost every physical prediction is bookkeeping on conserved quantities, which is why the subject is far more unified than school physics makes it appear.
#conservation #energy #fields #measurement

00 Why this domain exists, and the depth setting

Understanding constraints, not solving problem sets.

School physics is a sequence of problems with clean numbers. That is not this. The commander-level goal is to know which quantity is binding in any physical situation — energy, heat, materials or information — so you can reason about a device you have never seen.

N1 The method

The transferable part — worth more than any individual law.

WHAT IT IS. Three habits: checking units, estimating orders of magnitude before computing, and looking for the conserved quantity before looking for a formula.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because these three catch most errors and solve most problems approximately, which is usually enough. They also transfer to every other domain in the Atlas.

DEPENDENCIES. Mathematics N1.

SUBTOPICS. SI units and derived units · dimensional analysis as an error check · Fermi estimation · conservation laws as constraints · symmetry · significant figures and honest precision.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free online book · Title: The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Volume I chapters 1–4 — free in full at feynmanlectures.caltech.edu
Why this one: free, and Feynman opens by explaining what conservation means before any mathematics. Chapter 4 on energy is the single best thing written about the subject.
HANDS-ON · Derive a formula you have forgotten, from units alone You have forgotten the formula for the period of a pendulum. Recover it without looking: the period can only depend on length, gravity and mass. Work out which combination of those gives units of seconds. Mass cannot appear — no combination works. You get period ∝ √(L/g), which is correct up to a constant.

You just derived physics from bookkeeping. Then verify by timing a real pendulum — a phone on a string works — and solve for the missing constant. You will get something near 2π.

Then apply the method defensively: take three formulas from any domain and check their units. Then take a claim from a news article involving physical quantities and check whether it is dimensionally possible.

The realisation to reach: units are not bookkeeping overhead, they are a constraint strong enough to reconstruct lost knowledge and to kill wrong answers before you compute them.

CONNECTS TO. Mathematics N1 · Every engineering domain · Infrastructure N7 (estimation).

N2 Mechanics

Force, motion and energy — the part with the strongest everyday intuition, and several wrong ones.

WHAT IT IS. How forces change motion, and the conserved quantities — momentum and energy — that let you predict outcomes without tracking every force.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because it is the foundation of every mechanical device, and because human intuition about motion is systematically wrong in ways that were only corrected in the 17th century.

DEPENDENCIES. N1 · Mathematics N5.

SUBTOPICS. Newton's three laws · why objects in motion do not need a force · momentum and its conservation · impulse · work, kinetic and potential energy · power · friction · rotation, torque and angular momentum · centre of mass.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free course · Title: MIT OCW 8.01 Classical Mechanics (Walter Lewin's lecture videos and problem sets are free)
Why this one: free, and the lectures are built around live demonstrations — you watch the prediction get tested in the room, which is the correct order.
HANDS-ON · Measure gravity with a phone, then find where energy went Use your phone's accelerometer — free apps expose it — to measure g by dropping it onto something soft, or by timing a pendulum from N1. You should get within a few percent of 9.8 m/s².

Now the energy exercise. Roll a ball down a ramp of known height and measure its speed at the bottom (film it, count frames). Compute the kinetic energy you got, and the potential energy you started with. They will not match. The difference went into friction, rotation and sound.

Then quantify one of them: a rolling ball stores a predictable fraction of its energy in rotation, so it always arrives slower than a sliding one. Predict the ratio, then test with a ball and a sliding block on the same ramp. The energy you cannot see is still exactly accounted for — which is what conservation actually means in practice.

The realisation to reach: "energy was lost" is never true. It moved somewhere you were not measuring, and finding where is the whole method.

CONNECTS TO. Mechanical Engineering · Cars (braking, crash safety) · Biology (locomotion) · Systems Thinking (momentum as a stock).

N3 Thermodynamics

The domain's hardest constraint, and the one that limits every engine and every chip.

WHAT IT IS. The relationship between heat, work and energy, and the law stating that some conversions are permanently one-way.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because it sets absolute limits nothing can engineer around. No engine can exceed Carnot efficiency, no computation can be free of heat, and no process can decrease total entropy. These are not current limitations — they are permanent.

DEPENDENCIES. N2.

SUBTOPICS. Temperature versus heat versus internal energy · specific heat · the first law · the second law and entropy · reversibility · heat engines and Carnot efficiency · refrigeration and heat pumps · conduction, convection, radiation · thermal resistance.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free online book · Title: The Feynman Lectures, Volume I chapters 44–46 (heat engines, the second law, ratchets)
Why this one: free, and the ratchet-and-pawl chapter is the clearest demonstration anywhere of why you cannot cheat the second law — Feynman builds a machine that appears to and then shows exactly where it fails.
HANDS-ON · Measure the efficiency of your own kettle, then your room Boil a known mass of water from a known starting temperature. Time it, and get the kettle's power rating from its label. Compute the energy that should have been needed — mass × specific heat × temperature rise — and compare it to the energy actually consumed. The ratio is your kettle's efficiency, and it will be high because resistive heating is nearly perfect.

Then do the same for something that must obey Carnot: look up an air conditioner's rated coefficient of performance and compare it against the theoretical maximum for the temperatures involved. It will be a fraction of it. A heater can be 100% efficient; an engine or a cooler can never be. That asymmetry is the second law, and it is why waste heat is unavoidable.

Then measure your room's thermal behaviour: record temperature every ten minutes after switching off cooling, and fit the curve. You will get exponential decay towards ambient — the same shape as an RC circuit discharging, because it is the same differential equation. Compute the time constant, and you have measured your room's insulation.

The realisation to reach: heat is energy that has become disordered, and disorder is why the universe has a direction. Every efficiency number you will ever see is a distance from a limit set by two temperatures.

CONNECTS TO. Mechanical (engines) · Computing N4 (chips are thermally limited) · Cars · Biology (metabolism) · Geography (climate) · Systems Thinking (entropy as decay).

N4 Waves and optics

How energy and information travel without anything moving from A to B.

WHAT IT IS. Oscillations that propagate — sound, light, radio, water — and the behaviours common to all of them regardless of medium.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because every communication technology is a wave, every display is optics, and resonance explains everything from musical instruments to bridges collapsing to why your microwave uses the frequency it does.

DEPENDENCIES. N2.

SUBTOPICS. Frequency, wavelength, amplitude, phase · wave speed and the medium · superposition and interference · standing waves and resonance · the Doppler effect · reflection and refraction · lenses and focal length · diffraction · polarisation · the electromagnetic spectrum as one continuum.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free simulations · Title: PhET Interactive Simulations (University of Colorado) — "Wave Interference", "Bending Light", "Sound Waves", all free
Why this one: free, and waves are one of the few subjects where a manipulable simulation beats both text and real apparatus, because you can change one variable at a time and see the whole field.
HANDS-ON · Measure the speed of sound in your corridor Use a phone to record a sharp clap in a long corridor or against a distant wall. Open the recording in free audio software and measure the gap between the clap and its echo. Multiply by the distance and you have the speed of sound, typically within a few percent of 343 m/s.

Then do resonance: fill a glass with varying amounts of water and find the pitch each produces. Plot pitch against air column length. You have just measured a standing wave relationship, and you can now predict the note before you tap.

Then the one that explains screens and sunglasses: take two polarising filters — cheap, or salvage them from an old LCD panel — and rotate one against the other. Light disappears completely at 90°. Then look at your phone screen through one filter and rotate it: the screen goes black, because LCD output is already polarised. You have just discovered a fundamental property of how every display you own works, using a piece of plastic.

The realisation to reach: sound, light, radio and Wi-Fi differ only in frequency and medium. Everything you learn about one transfers exactly to the others.

CONNECTS TO. Electronics (signals, antennas) · Networking N2 (the physical layer) · Everyday Technology (displays, microwaves, remotes) · Biology (hearing, vision).

N5 Electromagnetism

The force responsible for essentially everything you interact with.

WHAT IT IS. The behaviour of electric charge — the fields it creates, the forces it exerts, the magnetism produced by its motion, and the induction that links the two.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because every chemical bond, every material property, every circuit, every motor and every wireless signal is electromagnetic. Gravity holds the planets; electromagnetism does everything else you encounter.

DEPENDENCIES. N2, N4.

SUBTOPICS. Charge, Coulomb's law, fields · potential and voltage · current · resistance and Ohm's law · magnetic fields from currents · the motor effect · electromagnetic induction and Faraday's law · transformers · why light is an electromagnetic wave · why AC won for distribution.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free video series · Title: Ben Eater's electronics and signals videos, alongside PhET's "Faraday's Law" and "Circuit Construction Kit" simulations — all free
Why this one: free, and Eater builds real circuits on breadboards while explaining the field behaviour. The pairing of simulation and physical construction is what makes this node stick.
HANDS-ON · Make electricity from a magnet, then make motion from electricity Wind a hundred turns of thin enamelled wire around a pen and connect the ends to a multimeter on its most sensitive voltage range. Move a small magnet in and out. Voltage appears. Move it faster and the voltage rises; hold it still inside the coil and the voltage is zero.

Change is what generates, not presence. That single observation is Faraday's law, it is the principle of every generator producing the electricity in your wall, and you just demonstrated it with two rupees of wire.

Now run it backwards: pass current through the same coil near a compass and watch the needle deflect. Electricity makes magnetism; changing magnetism makes electricity. That symmetry is the whole of generators, motors, transformers and induction cooktops.

Then find it in your home: open a phone charger's specifications and identify the transformer stage. Ask why it must run at high frequency to be small — and derive the answer from Faraday's law, since faster change means more voltage per turn, which means fewer turns and less iron.

The realisation to reach: electricity and magnetism are not two subjects. They are one field observed from two reference frames, and every electrical machine exploits the coupling.

CONNECTS TO. Electrical & Electronics (this is its foundation) · Cars (motors, alternators) · Everyday Technology · Networking (radio) · Biology (nerve conduction).

N6 Modern physics

Where intuition stops — take only what the rest of the Atlas needs.

WHAT IT IS. Quantisation, atomic structure, band theory, nuclear energy, and the two relativistic effects that have practical consequences.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because semiconductors, lasers, LEDs, solar cells, nuclear power and GPS all depend on it. You need the mechanism, not the mathematics.

DEPENDENCIES. N4, N5.

SUBTOPICS. Photons and the photoelectric effect · atomic energy levels · energy bands and the band gap · conductors, insulators, semiconductors · doping and the p-n junction · LEDs and photovoltaics · fission and fusion, binding energy, enrichment · special relativity's time dilation and general relativity's gravitational shift, and why GPS needs both.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free online book · Title: The Feynman Lectures, Volume III chapter 1 ("Quantum Behavior") — free
Why this one: free, and it presents the double-slit experiment as the single phenomenon containing the entire mystery. Feynman is explicit that nobody's intuition survives it, which is more useful than a treatment that pretends otherwise.
HANDS-ON · Measure Planck's constant with a handful of LEDs Get LEDs of several colours — red, yellow, green, blue — a variable power supply or a potentiometer, and a multimeter. For each LED, slowly raise the voltage and record the exact voltage at which it first emits light.

Red lights up at the lowest voltage, blue at the highest. Now plot turn-on voltage against the light's frequency (colour wavelength is printed on the datasheet, or look it up). You get a straight line, and its slope is Planck's constant divided by the electron charge.

Compute it. You will land within perhaps 10% of the real value. You have measured a fundamental constant of the universe on a breadboard for the price of a snack, and simultaneously demonstrated that light comes in discrete packets whose energy depends on frequency — which is the entire quantum revolution in one afternoon.

Then reason about consequences: why a solar cell has a maximum theoretical efficiency, why infrared cannot cause sunburn regardless of intensity, and why a blue LED was a Nobel-winning problem when red ones were easy.

The realisation to reach: quantisation is not philosophy. It is a measurable property with a slope you can determine yourself, and everything about semiconductors follows from it.

CONNECTS TO. Electronics (semiconductors are this node) · AI/Computing (the transistor) · Everyday Technology (LEDs, solar, screens) · Geopolitics (nuclear, enrichment) · Networking (GPS).
