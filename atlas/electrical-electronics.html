Electrical & Electronics

This is the bridge domain — the place where physics becomes computing. It is also the most hands-on-able domain in the Atlas: a few hundred rupees of components lets you build, measure and break things that make the abstractions permanent.

The single idea. Voltage is pressure, current is flow, resistance is the pipe. That analogy is imperfect but carries you a remarkably long way, and everything else — capacitors, transistors, logic gates — is a device that manipulates that flow in one specific way.
#ohms-law #transistors #logic #signals

00 Why this domain exists

And why almost none of the physics is actually required.

The "physics before electronics" prerequisite is largely false. Ohm's law, circuits, components and digital logic need arithmetic and a mental model of flow. Electromagnetic field theory becomes necessary only at antennas, transmission lines and high frequencies — which is far past commander-level.

01 The kit

One purchase makes this entire domain hands-on. It is the best value in the Atlas.

ESSENTIAL (a few hundred rupees, once)
  Breadboard + jumper wires
  Multimeter (any cheap digital one)
  Assorted resistors, capacitors, LEDs
  A few transistors (BC547) and diodes (1N4007)
  9V battery or a 5V USB supply

VERY USEFUL
  Arduino Uno or clone      — microcontroller, ADC, PWM
  Logic gate ICs (7400 series)
  Potentiometers, push buttons

OPTIONAL BUT TRANSFORMATIVE
  USB oscilloscope or logic analyser  — makes signals visible
  Old device to disassemble           — free, and the best teacher
Safety, stated once and meant Everything in this domain is done at battery or USB voltages. Mains electricity kills. Do not open power supplies, do not probe live mains, and do not disassemble anything containing a large capacitor — a CRT or a microwave oven can hold a lethal charge for a long time after unplugging. Every experiment on this page works safely below 12 volts.

N1 The three quantities

Voltage, current, resistance — and the relationship between them.

WHAT IT IS. Voltage is the energy difference per unit charge between two points. Current is the rate of charge flow. Resistance is the opposition to that flow. Ohm's law relates all three.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because nearly every electrical question reduces to these three plus power. Getting them genuinely intuitive — including that voltage is always between two points and never at one — resolves most confusion permanently.

DEPENDENCIES. Arithmetic.

SUBTOPICS. Charge and current · voltage as potential difference · resistance and resistivity · Ohm's law · power = VI · energy and the kilowatt-hour · why "voltage at a point" is meaningless without a reference · ground as a chosen zero.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free simulation · Title: PhET "Circuit Construction Kit: DC" — free, browser-based
Why this one: free, and it shows current as animated moving charge with meters you can attach anywhere. Build the circuit in simulation, predict, then build it physically and check.
HANDS-ON · Kill an LED, then work out why Connect an LED directly across a 9V battery. It flashes brightly and dies. Now work out why. An LED has a nearly fixed forward voltage of about 2V and almost no resistance beyond it — so with 9V applied, current is limited only by whatever tiny resistance exists, and it is enormous.

Compute the resistor needed to limit current to 20 mA: (9 − 2) / 0.02 = 350Ω. Use the nearest value, rebuild, and it works. You have just used Ohm's law to solve a real problem you created, which is worth more than twenty textbook problems.

Then measure everything with your multimeter to confirm the model: voltage across the resistor, voltage across the LED, and — the one people get wrong — current, which requires breaking the circuit and putting the meter in series. Confirm the two voltages sum to 9V, and that the same current flows through both components.

Then compute power dissipated in the resistor, touch it, and notice it is slightly warm. That warmth is the energy your calculation predicted.

The realisation to reach: components do not "use" voltage, they drop it, and the drops must sum to the source. Current is the same everywhere in a single loop. Those two rules solve most circuits.

CONNECTS TO. Physics N5 · Computing (power and heat limits) · Cars (12V systems).

N2 Circuits

How components combine, and the two configurations everything reduces to.

WHAT IT IS. Series and parallel arrangements, the laws governing them, and the standard sub-circuits — dividers, RC networks — that appear inside every real device.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because real circuits are large, and analysing them requires reducing them to combinations of two patterns.

DEPENDENCIES. N1.

SUBTOPICS. Series and parallel · Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws · voltage dividers · current dividers · RC time constants · charging and discharging curves · loading effects · why measurement changes the circuit.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free simulation + free videos · Title: PhET Circuit Construction Kit, alongside Ben Eater's breadboard series
Why this one: both free, and the combination lets you predict in simulation and verify in hardware, which is exactly the prediction-then-measurement discipline used throughout this Atlas.
HANDS-ON · Build a clock out of a resistor and a capacitor Wire a resistor and capacitor in series across a supply, with a voltmeter across the capacitor. Close the circuit and watch the voltage rise — not linearly, but along a curve that flattens.

Record voltage every second and plot it. It is exponential, and the time to reach 63% of the supply is exactly R × C. Change R, then change C, and confirm the time constant scales as predicted. You have built a timer out of two passive components with no clock and no code.

Then recognise the shape: it is identical to the room-cooling curve from Physics N3, because both are the same differential equation — a quantity approaching equilibrium at a rate proportional to the remaining gap. That is also a balancing loop.

Then use it: this circuit is how a button debounce works, how a camera flash charges, and how the simplest oscillators set their frequency.

The realisation to reach: a capacitor is a small energy reservoir, and pairing it with a resistor creates time — a physical delay from nothing but two components.

CONNECTS TO. Systems Thinking N1–N3 (a capacitor is a stock, RC is a delay) · Physics N3 (identical maths) · Computing N2.

N3 Components

Six devices. Learn what each one does to flow, and you can read any schematic.

WHAT IT IS. The passive and simple active components — resistor, capacitor, inductor, diode, plus switches and sensors — and the single job each performs.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because a schematic is a sentence and components are its vocabulary. A small vocabulary covers the overwhelming majority of circuits.

DEPENDENCIES. N2.

SUBTOPICS. Resistors and colour codes · capacitors and charge storage · inductors and opposition to current change · diodes and one-way flow · rectification · LEDs · sensors as variable resistors · reading a datasheet.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free primary documents · Title: component datasheets — free from every manufacturer
Why this one: free, authoritative, and learning to read a datasheet is the actual professional skill. Take a BC547 transistor datasheet and find its maximum current, gain range and package pinout. Everything else in electronics assumes you can do this.
HANDS-ON · Open a dead device and identify every component Take apart something broken and cheap — an old charger with the mains section left alone, a broken toy, a dead remote, a discarded router. Identify every component on the board and state what job it does.

You will find the same small set repeatedly: resistors limiting current, capacitors smoothing supply, diodes preventing reverse current, an inductor in the power section, a crystal setting a clock, and one or two chips doing everything else. Look up the markings on the largest chip — datasheets are free — and you will often find the entire device's function described in its first paragraph.

Then trace the power path with your eyes: from the input, through protection, through regulation, to the chip. Every board has this path, and once you can find it you can orient yourself in any electronics you ever open.

The realisation to reach: electronics is not infinitely varied. It is a small vocabulary reused, and the difference between devices is mostly the chip in the middle and the software inside it.

CONNECTS TO. Everyday Technology (this is the disassembly skill) · Cars (automotive electronics) · Physics N5.

N4 Semiconductors and the transistor

The single most consequential invention in the Atlas.

WHAT IT IS. Materials whose conductivity can be controlled, and the devices built from them — principally the transistor, which lets a small signal control a large one.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because control requires amplification and switching. A transistor does both, it has no moving parts, and it can be made microscopically small — which is why there are more transistors in existence than any other manufactured object in history.

DEPENDENCIES. N3 · Physics N6 (band gaps).

SUBTOPICS. Doping, n-type and p-type · the p-n junction and why it conducts one way · the transistor as a switch · the transistor as an amplifier · saturation and cut-off · MOSFETs versus BJTs · why switching produces heat · integration and Moore's law.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free video series · Title: Ben Eater's transistor and logic gate videos, free on YouTube
Why this one: free, and he builds logic gates from discrete transistors on a breadboard before ever using a logic IC. Seeing a NAND gate made of three transistors permanently removes the magic from computing.
HANDS-ON · Control a large current with a tiny one, then build a gate Wire a transistor as a switch: a small current into the base, a much larger current through the collector driving an LED or a small motor. Measure both currents. The ratio is the gain, typically 100 to 300. A current too small to light the LED is controlling one that easily does.

Now sweep the base current slowly with a potentiometer and watch the output. In the middle region it is an amplifier — output proportional to input. Push it to either extreme and it becomes a switch — fully on or fully off. Same device, two uses, and the choice of operating region is the entire distinction between analog and digital electronics.

Then build a NAND gate from transistors and resistors, and verify its truth table with a multimeter. Then connect two NAND gates in the feedback arrangement from Computing N2 and watch it hold a bit. You have built memory out of transistors, having earlier built it in a simulator — and now you know what the simulator was simulating.

The realisation to reach: everything above this in the Atlas — logic, computers, software, AI — is this one device, made small and repeated billions of times.

CONNECTS TO. Computing N2 (this is the layer below it) · Physics N6 · Geopolitics (semiconductor supply chains) · AI (compute is transistors).

N5 Digital logic

Where this domain hands off to Computing.

WHAT IT IS. Treating voltages as two symbols and building combinational and sequential circuits that compute functions and store state.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because analog signals degrade with every operation while digital ones can be restored to clean levels at every stage. Digitisation trades resolution for noise immunity, and that trade is why computers work at all.

DEPENDENCIES. N4 · Mathematics N3 (boolean logic).

SUBTOPICS. Logic levels and noise margins · gates and truth tables · combinational logic · adders · latches and flip-flops · clocks and sequential logic · registers and counters · propagation delay and why clock speed has a limit.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free project course · Title: Nand2Tetris projects 1–3 (free), paired with physical 7400-series logic ICs
Why this one: free, and doing it both in simulation and in hardware closes the loop between Computing N2 and this node — the same construction, once in software and once in wire.
HANDS-ON · Build a 4-bit adder and watch it be wrong at speed Using 7400-series ICs, build a full adder, then chain four to make a 4-bit adder with LEDs on the output. Set inputs with switches and verify the arithmetic by hand.

Now find the limit. Each gate takes time to settle — nanoseconds — and in a ripple-carry adder the carry must propagate through every stage before the answer is valid. If you sampled the output too early you would read garbage that briefly appears as the carry ripples through.

That propagation delay is precisely why processors have a maximum clock speed, and why faster chips need shorter logic paths rather than merely faster clocks. If you have a logic analyser, capture the transition and see the intermediate wrong values that exist for a few nanoseconds.

Then measure noise immunity directly: feed a gate an input voltage between the two logic levels and watch the output snap decisively to one or the other. That snapping is the restoration that makes digital signals survivable across long chains.

The realisation to reach: digital is not the absence of analog. It is analog with thresholds and a restoration step, and every digital abstraction you rely on has an analog reality underneath with a settling time.

CONNECTS TO. Computing N2–N3 (the handoff) · Mathematics N3 · Everyday Technology.

N6 Signals and conversion

How the continuous world enters and leaves a digital device.

WHAT IT IS. Sampling analog quantities into numbers, converting numbers back into analog outputs, and modulating signals for transmission.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because reality is continuous and computers are not. Every microphone, camera, sensor, speaker and radio crosses this boundary, and the crossing has rules with consequences.

DEPENDENCIES. N5 · Physics N4.

SUBTOPICS. Sampling rate and the Nyquist limit · aliasing · quantisation and bit depth · ADCs and DACs · PWM as fake analog · filtering · amplitude and frequency modulation · digital modulation · signal-to-noise ratio · why bandwidth and data rate are related.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free tool + free documentation · Title: Audacity (free) for visualising real signals, with an Arduino's ADC and PWM documentation
Why this one: free, and Audacity lets you generate, record and inspect waveforms so that sampling artefacts become visible and audible rather than theoretical.
HANDS-ON · Make a high note become a low one In Audacity, generate a 3,000 Hz tone. Now resample the project down to 4,000 Hz — below twice the tone frequency. Play it. The tone is now a completely different, lower pitch. It did not degrade; it became something else entirely.

That is aliasing, and you have just demonstrated the Nyquist limit: sampling below twice the highest frequency does not lose the signal, it disguises it as a different one. This is why anti-aliasing filters exist before every ADC, and why the same effect makes wagon wheels appear to spin backwards in film.

Then do quantisation: reduce a recording from 16-bit to 8-bit to 4-bit and listen. The hiss you hear is quantisation noise — the error between the true value and the nearest representable one, which is Computing N1's floating point problem in audible form.

Then build PWM on an Arduino: drive an LED with a fast on-off square wave and vary the duty cycle. The brightness varies smoothly even though the output is only ever fully on or fully off. Your eye is doing the averaging — and this is how nearly all digital dimming and motor speed control works.

The realisation to reach: sampling is not approximation, it is exact if the rate is high enough — and catastrophically wrong rather than merely imprecise if it is not.

CONNECTS TO. Computing N1 (quantisation) · Networking N2 (modulation) · Everyday Technology (audio, cameras, remotes) · AI (all sensor data crosses this boundary).

N7 Power and the grid

Where electricity comes from, and the largest machine ever built.

WHAT IT IS. Generation, transmission and distribution of electrical energy, and the reason alternating current won.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because energy must be generated where fuel or wind is and consumed where people are, and moving it costs. Transformers make high-voltage, low-loss transmission possible, and they only work with alternating current — which settled a commercial war with a physics argument.

DEPENDENCIES. N1, N3 · Physics N5 (induction).

SUBTOPICS. AC and DC · why I²R losses force high transmission voltage · transformers · three phase · the grid as a real-time balancing system · frequency as the balance indicator · inertia · why renewables complicate stability · batteries and storage · India's grid specifics.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free live data + free videos · Title: India's national grid frequency and load data, published free by POSOCO/Grid-India, alongside Practical Engineering's free grid videos
Why this one: free, and watching real-time frequency data makes the grid's balancing act concrete: frequency is the visible evidence of supply and demand matching, second by second, across a continent.
HANDS-ON · Watch a country balance itself in real time Pull India's grid frequency data. Nominal is 50 Hz. Watch it drift — a few hundredths above when generation exceeds demand, below when it lags. There is no storage buffer. Generation must equal consumption continuously, and frequency is the error signal.

This is a balancing loop with a very short time constant, and the rotating mass of every large generator provides inertia that slows the error's growth long enough for control systems to respond. Then reason about why replacing spinning turbines with inverter-based solar reduces that inertia and makes the loop harder to control — one of the genuinely hard problems in energy transition, and now visible to you as a control problem rather than a political one.

Then do the transmission arithmetic: compute I²R losses for transmitting 100 MW at 400 V versus 400 kV over the same line resistance. The high-voltage case loses a millionth as much. That single ratio is why transformers exist, why AC won, and why every long-distance line runs at extreme voltage.

Then look at your own electricity bill, convert your monthly consumption to average watts, and compare it to a single incandescent bulb. Most people are surprised in one direction or the other.

The realisation to reach: the grid is a continuously controlled dynamic system with no storage — arguably the largest and most consequential control loop humans have built.

CONNECTS TO. Systems Thinking N2–N3 · Physics N3, N5 · Geopolitics (energy dependence) · Economics (electricity pricing and subsidy) · Cars (EV charging load).
