Artificial Intelligence

Strip away the vocabulary and this domain contains one idea repeated at increasing scale: define a measure of wrongness, then adjust a very large number of parameters to reduce it. Everything else — architectures, attention, scaling laws — is engineering around that single loop.

The single idea. A model does not learn facts. It fits a function to data by descending a gradient, and every capability and every failure follows from that. It has no notion of truth, only of loss — which is why it can be fluent and wrong at the same time without any internal signal that anything is amiss.

00 Why this domain exists, and the honest depth setting

Commander-level here means you can evaluate claims — not that you can train a frontier model.

This is the domain with the widest gap between public discourse and mechanism. The goal here is that you can hear a claim about AI and know which of four things it is: a genuine capability, a benchmark artefact, an economic argument, or marketing. That requires mechanism, not familiarity.

Gated byGates
Mathematics N4–N5 · Probability N2–N5 · Data EngineeringGeopolitics (compute) · Business (AI economics) · Psychology (comparisons to cognition)
The prerequisite people skip and then suffer for Data Engineering. Model quality is bounded by data quality, and the overwhelming majority of real AI work is pipelines, labels and evaluation sets. Studying architectures before data is how people end up able to describe a transformer and unable to build anything that works.

N1 Learning as optimisation

One loop. If you understand this node, the rest is elaboration.

WHAT IT IS. Define a function with adjustable parameters, define a loss measuring how wrong its outputs are, compute the gradient of that loss with respect to each parameter, and step in the opposite direction. Repeat.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because for most interesting problems nobody knows the rule, but everybody has examples. Optimisation converts "I cannot write the rule" into "I can measure being wrong", which is a problem computers can attack.

DEPENDENCIES. Mathematics N5 (derivatives), N4 (vectors).

SUBTOPICS. Parameters and hypothesis space · loss functions and what each one rewards · gradient descent, stochastic and mini-batch · learning rate · epochs · local minima and saddle points · why non-convexity turned out to matter less than expected.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free video + code · Title: Andrej Karpathy, Neural Networks: Zero to Hero, episode 1 ("The spelled-out intro to backpropagation") — free on YouTube with free code
Why this one: free, and it builds the entire mechanism from scratch in Python with no framework. You end the episode having written an autograd engine, which means gradient descent stops being a metaphor.
HANDS-ON · Fit a line with no library, then destroy it Generate points along a noisy line. Now fit it with gradient descent written by hand — two parameters, a squared-error loss, derivatives you derive yourself, a loop. Print the loss each step and watch it fall.

Now break it three ways and understand each. Raise the learning rate until the loss increases instead of decreasing, then keeps increasing, then becomes NaN — you have made the steps larger than the valley is wide. Lower it until convergence would take a million steps. Remove feature scaling by making one input a thousand times larger, and watch the loss surface become a narrow ravine that gradient descent bounces across instead of descending.

Then plot the loss surface itself for your two parameters as a contour map and trace the path your optimiser took across it. The abstract idea "descend the gradient" becomes a picture of a ball rolling down a valley, and every hyperparameter becomes a physical property of that valley.

The realisation to reach: training is not mysterious and it is not intelligent. It is a ball rolling downhill in a space with a great many dimensions, and most practical difficulty is about the shape of the hill.

CONNECTS TO. Mathematics N5 · Economics (constrained optimisation) · Systems Thinking (a feedback loop converging on a set point).

N2 Classical machine learning

Where the concepts that still govern everything were established — and often the right tool.

WHAT IT IS. The family of methods predating deep learning: regression, decision trees and ensembles, clustering — together with the conceptual apparatus of generalisation that all of AI still uses.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because for tabular data with modest sample sizes — which describes most real business problems — a gradient-boosted tree still beats a neural network, trains in seconds, and can be explained to a regulator.

DEPENDENCIES. N1 · Mathematics N6 · Probability N3.

SUBTOPICS. Supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement · linear and logistic regression · decision trees, random forests, gradient boosting · k-means and hierarchical clustering · overfitting and underfitting · the bias-variance trade-off · regularisation · cross-validation · feature engineering.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free documentation and user guide · Title: the scikit-learn User Guide
Why this one: free, and unusually good — it is a genuine textbook disguised as documentation, with the assumptions and failure modes of each method stated plainly next to runnable code.

DEEPER (optional) — An Introduction to Statistical Learning (James et al.), free PDF from the authors, for the statistical reasoning underneath.
HANDS-ON · Achieve 100% accuracy and learn nothing Take a small real dataset. Split it into train and test. Fit a decision tree with unlimited depth. Training accuracy: 100%. Test accuracy: considerably worse.

Now plot both against tree depth, from 1 to 30, on the same axes. Training accuracy climbs monotonically to perfection. Test accuracy climbs, peaks, and then falls. The gap between the curves is overfitting made visible, and the peak is the depth you should have chosen.

Then run the experiment that reframes the whole domain: shuffle your labels at random, destroying any real relationship, and train the same unlimited-depth tree. It still achieves 100% training accuracy. A model can perfectly memorise pure noise. Fitting the training data proves nothing whatsoever.

Finally, hold out a test set, then tune twenty hyperparameters against it, then report the test score. Then get a genuinely fresh test set and watch the score drop. You have just leaked information through your own decisions — the same mechanism as multiple comparisons in Mathematics N6.

The realisation to reach: every number a model reports about itself is optimistic, and the discipline of holding data back is the only defence.

CONNECTS TO. Mathematics N6 · Probability N5 (generalisation is a sampling question) · Psychology (the replication crisis is overfitting in another field).

N3 Neural networks and backpropagation depth

Composition plus the chain rule. That is the entire innovation.

WHAT IT IS. Functions built by composing many simple parameterised transformations with nonlinearities between them, trained by propagating the gradient backwards through the composition using the chain rule.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because feature engineering does not scale. Classical methods need a human to decide what to measure; a deep network learns the intermediate representations itself, given enough data and compute.

DEPENDENCIES. N1 · Mathematics N4–N5.

SUBTOPICS. Neurons and layers · activation functions and why nonlinearity is essential · the forward pass · backpropagation as the chain rule applied in reverse · why depth beats width · vanishing and exploding gradients · normalisation · residual connections · dropout.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free video series + code · Title: Karpathy, Neural Networks: Zero to Hero, episodes 1–4
Why this one: free, and it is the single best resource in this Atlas for its subject. You build micrograd — a working automatic differentiation engine — in about a hundred lines, and afterwards PyTorch contains no mysteries because you have written the part that seemed magical.

DEEPER (optional) — 3Blue1Brown's neural network series (free) for geometric intuition, and Dive into Deep Learning (d2l.ai, free) for breadth of architectures.
HANDS-ON · Remove the nonlinearity and watch depth become useless Build a two-layer network by hand and train it on a problem that is not linearly separable — XOR is the classic and takes four data points. It works.

Now delete the activation functions, leaving pure linear layers, and retrain. It fails completely, and the reason is worth deriving yourself: a composition of linear transformations is itself a single linear transformation. Ten layers without nonlinearity have exactly the expressive power of one. Verify it by multiplying the weight matrices together and confirming you get an equivalent single matrix — which is Mathematics N4 arriving with consequences.

Then induce a vanishing gradient deliberately: stack twenty layers with sigmoid activations and print the gradient magnitude at each layer. It shrinks geometrically towards the input, so early layers stop learning. Swap to ReLU and add residual connections, and watch the gradients survive. You have just re-derived two of the innovations that made deep learning possible.

The realisation to reach: the architecture choices that look arbitrary in a paper are almost all fixes for specific, observable training pathologies — and you can observe every one of them on a laptop.

CONNECTS TO. Mathematics N4–N5 · Computing N4 (training is memory-bandwidth bound) · Biology (the analogy to neurons is loose and worth knowing the limits of).

N4 Representations and embeddings

Turning meaning into geometry — and the idea that makes modern search and retrieval work.

WHAT IT IS. Mapping discrete things — words, images, users, products — into vectors positioned so that similar things are close together, learned rather than designed.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because models operate on numbers, and one-hot encoding destroys all relationships. An embedding preserves them, so that similarity becomes distance and the geometry carries the semantics.

DEPENDENCIES. N3 · Mathematics N4 (dot product as similarity).

SUBTOPICS. One-hot encoding and its poverty · learned embeddings · the distributional hypothesis · cosine similarity · analogical structure · transfer learning and fine-tuning · vector databases and approximate nearest neighbour · retrieval-augmented generation.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free video + free paper · Title: 3Blue1Brown's "Word embeddings" explanation, followed by the original word2vec paper
Why this one: both free. The video gives the geometric picture and the paper shows how astonishingly simple the training objective was — predict a word from its neighbours — which makes the emergent structure more striking, not less.
HANDS-ON · Do arithmetic on meaning, then find where it breaks Load a set of pretrained word embeddings. Compute king − man + woman and find the nearest vector. You get queen, and it is genuinely startling the first time — arithmetic on meaning.

Now hunt for failures, which is the more educational half. Try analogies across domains, with rare words, with words having several senses. Bank collapses river and finance into one point because the model saw one string. Try doctor − man + woman and observe what the training corpus believed about the world. The bias is not a bug in the algorithm; it is a faithful measurement of the text it was trained on.

Then build something real and small: embed a few hundred of your own documents, implement cosine similarity by hand, and build a search that finds documents by meaning rather than keyword. Compare it against simple keyword search on ten queries and find the cases where each wins. Neither dominates, and knowing why is the practical skill.

The realisation to reach: these models learn the structure of their corpus, faithfully, including everything in it you would rather they had not learned.

CONNECTS TO. Mathematics N4 · Databases (vector indexes) · Human Behavior (bias in text is bias in society, measured) · Communication (meaning is contextual).

N5 Attention and transformers

The current architecture, and the specific problem it solved.

WHAT IT IS. A mechanism that lets every position in a sequence look directly at every other position, weighting them by learned relevance — removing the sequential bottleneck of earlier models.

WHY IT EXISTS. Recurrent models processed sequences one step at a time, which made them slow to train and poor at long-range dependencies. Attention is parallelisable across the sequence, which is what allowed training to scale to the datasets that produced current capabilities.

DEPENDENCIES. N3, N4 · Mathematics N4.

SUBTOPICS. The sequence problem · queries, keys and values · scaled dot-product attention · why the scaling factor exists · multi-head attention · positional encoding, since attention is order-blind · encoder and decoder stacks · autoregressive generation · context length and its quadratic cost · KV caching.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free video + code · Title: Karpathy, "Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out" — free, with free code
Why this one: free, and you build a working transformer in a couple of hundred lines and train it on a small text file on a laptop. Attention stops being a diagram the moment you have written the matrix multiply.

DEEPER (optional) — "Attention Is All You Need" (free), readable directly once you have built the thing it describes.
HANDS-ON · Compute one attention matrix by hand, then remove position Take five tokens. Assign each a small random embedding. Compute queries, keys and values with three small matrices, then compute the attention matrix yourself — dot products, scale, softmax. Print it as a 5×5 grid and read off which token attended to which.

Now remove the scaling factor and recompute with larger embedding dimensions. The softmax saturates, attention collapses onto a single token, and gradients vanish. That innocuous √d in the formula is preventing a specific numerical failure, and you have just reproduced it.

Then the most revealing experiment: delete positional encoding and train a small model. It produces text with plausible vocabulary and no grammar, because attention is a set operation — it has no inherent notion of order. Word order, in a transformer, is entirely an additive hack.

Finally, measure the quadratic cost: time a forward pass at context lengths 128, 256, 512, 1024. Plot it. The curve is quadratic, and you have just explained why context windows are expensive and why every long-context technique exists.

The realisation to reach: a transformer is a stack of matrix multiplications with a clever weighting scheme. It is not reasoning, and knowing precisely what it is makes claims about it far easier to evaluate.

CONNECTS TO. Mathematics N4 · Computing N4 (memory bandwidth and KV cache) · Infrastructure (GPU scheduling) · Geopolitics (the compute buildout).

N6 The economics of models

Where this domain meets Value & Exchange, and where most strategy discussions actually live.

WHAT IT IS. The cost structure of training and running models: FLOPs, memory bandwidth, the scaling relationship between compute, data and performance, and the techniques that reduce inference cost.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because capability is now purchased with compute, which makes AI strategy a capital allocation question. It is also the node that explains why inference — not training — dominates the bill for anyone actually operating a product.

DEPENDENCIES. N3, N5 · Computing N4 · Infrastructure N7.

SUBTOPICS. FLOPs as the unit of training cost · memory bandwidth as the real inference bottleneck · scaling laws · compute-optimal training · quantisation · distillation · batching and throughput versus latency · cost per token · why inference dominates at scale.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free paper · Title: "Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models" (the Chinchilla paper), freely available
Why this one: free, and it is the clearest demonstration that these systems obey measurable economic relationships rather than magic. It also corrected a widely held wrong belief about model size versus data, which is a useful lesson in itself.
HANDS-ON · Compute the cost of a sentence For a model with N parameters, a forward pass costs roughly 2N floating point operations per token. Take a 7-billion-parameter model and compute the FLOPs to generate 500 tokens. Now look up the throughput of a GPU you could actually rent, and its hourly price. Compute the cost per thousand tokens.

Compare your figure to published API pricing. You will usually land within an order of magnitude, and the difference tells you something real about batching efficiency and margin.

Then find the bottleneck, which is not what most people assume. For single-request inference the GPU is mostly idle, waiting on memory — every parameter must be read for every token. Inference is memory-bandwidth bound, not compute bound, which is Computing N4 determining the economics of an entire industry. Verify by increasing batch size and watching throughput rise dramatically while latency barely changes.

Finally, quantise a small model from 16-bit to 8-bit or 4-bit. Measure memory, speed and quality. You will typically find memory falls by half or three-quarters with barely measurable quality loss — and you now understand why every deployment does this.

The realisation to reach: the constraints on AI are physical and financial, not mystical, and they can be estimated on the back of an envelope by anyone willing to do the arithmetic.

CONNECTS TO. Computing N4 · Infrastructure N7 · Business (gross margin of an AI product) · Geopolitics (chips, fabs, export controls) · Economics (capital intensity).

N7 Evaluation

The node that decides whether anything else was real.

WHAT IT IS. Measuring whether a model works — choosing metrics that match the actual objective, holding out data honestly, and recognising when a benchmark has stopped measuring anything.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because a model always produces an output, and outputs look like answers. Without disciplined evaluation there is no signal at all distinguishing a useful system from a confident one.

DEPENDENCIES. N2 · Probability N2, N5 · Mathematics N6.

SUBTOPICS. Train, validation and test splits · leakage · precision, recall, F1 · the precision-recall trade-off · ROC and AUC · accuracy's uselessness under class imbalance · confusion matrices · calibration · benchmark contamination · offline metrics versus online outcomes.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free documentation · Title: the scikit-learn model evaluation guide
Why this one: free, and it explains what each metric optimises for and when it misleads — which is the part omitted from almost every tutorial, and the only part that matters.
HANDS-ON · Build a 99% accurate model that is completely useless Take a problem where 1% of cases are positive — fraud, disease, defects. Write a "model" that always predicts negative. Compute its accuracy: 99%. It has never once identified the thing you built it to find.

Now compute precision, recall, and the confusion matrix, and watch them expose it immediately. Then train a real classifier and sweep its decision threshold from 0 to 1, plotting precision against recall. You cannot maximise both, and choosing a point on that curve is a business decision about the relative cost of a false positive and a false negative — not a technical one.

Then connect it to Probability N2, which you have already done: at 1% prevalence, even a highly accurate classifier produces mostly false positives among its alerts. Compute how many. The base rate defeats the classifier, and no amount of model improvement changes that arithmetic.

Finally, test calibration: bucket predictions by confidence and check whether the ones predicted at 70% confidence are actually right 70% of the time. Most models are badly overconfident, and knowing that changes how you should treat any probability a model reports.

The realisation to reach: the metric is the objective. A model optimises exactly what you measured, including all the ways your measurement differed from what you wanted.

CONNECTS TO. Probability N2 · Infrastructure N5 (alert precision) · Business (choosing the threshold is choosing a cost trade-off) · Psychology (overconfidence in humans too).

N8 Limits

What these systems structurally cannot do — the node that makes you useful in an argument.

WHAT IT IS. The failure modes that follow from the mechanism rather than from insufficient engineering: fabrication, distribution shift, inherited bias, and opacity.

WHY IT EXISTS. Because a model trained to produce likely continuations has no representation of truth. Fluency and accuracy are separate properties, and the system has no internal signal distinguishing them. Hallucination is not a defect layered on top of the mechanism; it is the mechanism, viewed from a case where it failed.

DEPENDENCIES. N5, N7.

SUBTOPICS. Fabrication as a structural property · distribution shift and silent degradation · bias from data, labels and objectives · interpretability and its current limits · adversarial examples · prompt injection as a security boundary problem · anthropomorphism as a reasoning error.

PRIMARY RESOURCE
Type: Free paper · Title: "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" (Bender et al.), plus the free model cards published for major models
Why this one: both free. The paper is the most cited critical treatment and is worth reading as an argument you evaluate rather than accept — which is itself the skill this Atlas is for. Model cards give the vendors' own account of limitations, which is often more candid than their marketing.
HANDS-ON · Get a confident, specific, entirely fabricated answer Ask a language model for citations on a narrow topic you know well. Then verify every one. Some will be real, some will have real authors attached to papers that do not exist, and some will be complete inventions with plausible titles, journals and years.

The important observation is not that it was wrong. It is that the fabricated citations are indistinguishable in tone and confidence from the real ones. There is no signal, because internally there is no difference — both are likely continuations.

Then measure variance: send the identical prompt twenty times and compare. Then measure stated confidence against actual correctness on twenty questions with checkable answers, and plot the calibration curve from N7.

Finally, test the security boundary: put an instruction inside a document and ask a model to summarise it. If it follows the embedded instruction, you have demonstrated prompt injection — data and instructions travel in the same channel, which is the same class of flaw as SQL injection and buffer overflows. It is not currently solved.

The realisation to reach: the failure modes are predictable from the mechanism. Once you know what the system is optimising, you can anticipate where it will fail — which is exactly what lets you use it well rather than either over-trusting or dismissing it.

CONNECTS TO. World Philosophy (truth, justification, what a claim is) · Psychology (fluency mistaken for competence, in machines and people) · Human Behavior (automation bias) · Security.
